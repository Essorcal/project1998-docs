The tick sweeps a viewer's viewport only when something it reads has changed (PR #269, refs the PR #266 pooling report)

A viewer whose map count and own pending flag are unchanged skips its three sweeps.
  • Release, 400 players and 305 mobs on one map with nobody moving: 2,100,116 ns a beat to 10,062 ns, 208 times less. Debug 7,534,610 to 25,304, 298 times. What is left is the snapshot alone.
  • The key is per map, so one walker anywhere un-skips every viewer of that map. On the walking spread it is worth nothing; it is for standing populations and the many quiet maps a live server has. With 5% of players stepping, or 5% of mobs, the two arms are inside the run spread, so the bookkeeping costs nothing measurable; the head allocates 120 B a beat more, the snapshot tuple growing by two fields.

Measurement: what a hold on World._lock costs (branch pr/lock-contention, no product change; laptop, harness in the review evidence)

  • 400 walkers and the real tick on one map, 700 accepted steps a second: the lock is held 1.8% of the time in Release (2.5% Debug), the tick holds 1.5 to 1.8% of that, and the view snapshot is 0.04 ms/s of it. The tick's own wait is 0.0002 ms/s. A walker finds the lock taken on about 7% of steps and waits 0 µs at the median and 35 µs at p99 Release; turning the tick off entirely changes the walkers' wait by 0.11 ms/s Debug and by nothing Release can resolve.
  • The whole snapshot family's ceiling is about 0.06 ms/s of walker wait, 0.05% of the session threads' step time. This answers PR #266's follow-up 4: no more snapshot slices.
  • The durable rule: wait grows with the square of a hold's length, not with the lock's total held time. The tick is 1.8% of the held time but about 40% of the wait, because its hold is one 0.28 ms block where a walker's are about 12 µs each. Tenfolding the tick's hold to 2.7 ms multiplied the walkers' wait by 16 (Debug, 0.28 to 4.40 ms/s). A 0.3 ms hold is free; a 3 ms hold is not.
  • Two laptop artefacts every in-process timing has to control for: CPU idle states (step median 598 µs against 131 µs with one core held awake) and the 15.6 ms Windows timer, which made all 400 walker threads wake on the same boundaries (57% contended fraction against 5% with timeBeginPeriod(1)).
  • The harness drove the tick at 600 ms; at the configured 333 ms its per-second tick figures scale by 1.8, so the tick's hold is about 0.5 ms/s Release rather than 0.29. Fourteen runs with a foreign build starting or finishing inside them were discarded and repeated; the discard compares process identities, not counts.
  • The rule goes into docs/common/Locking.md as the next docs slice.

The map broadcast helpers stop allocating: one call to 400 players goes from 45,361 bytes to 0 (PR #253, refs #250)

  • The two helpers every mob move, turn, swing, effect and player action goes through to reach the players on a map allocated a closure for every peer on every call, 96 bytes each, plus a LINQ copy of the player list. At 400 players on a map that was 45,361 bytes and 12.9 µs per call. The per-peer send now goes through the same static-lambda isolation the tick got in #250, and the player-list copy is rented from the shared array pool and handed back wiped, so a call allocates nothing of its own and takes 2.7 µs.
